# D&D x Good Society Hybrid: Rules v1.2 (draft)

**Table:** 1 DM + 3 players. Each player has 1 PC and builds 2 NPCs (one tied to each of the other two PCs) and plays them.
**Ratio:** about 90% D&D rules, about 10% Good Society. D&D handles combat, checks, spells and exploration. Good Society handles who the characters are, who they are tied to, and what society says about them.
**Setting:** Corvane, a high fantasy world with Regency-style politics: many noble houses and families of wealth and means. The Concord is the Oath and way of life every noble lives by. See the Deck for the desire, relationship and connection cards. Players have a separate, shorter cheat sheet; this book is the DM's full version.
**Campaign shape:** PCs start at **level 5** as established figures and level by **milestone** up to **level 20**. See section 12.
**Assumptions (change any of these):** D&D 5e.

---

## 1. Session Zero (from Collaboration)
Decide together, and write down the answers:
- **Tone:** drama, romantic comedy or farce.
- **Social vs. adventure balance:** roughly what mix of intrigue and dungeon/combat the group wants.
- **Review:** revisit these answers at every milestone.

## 2. Character Creation
1. **Desire.** Each player picks a secret desire card (#1-22). Players must act on it. DM can read all desires; other players cannot (unless the table chooses otherwise).
2. **Relationships.** The 3 PCs are linked in a ring using relationship cards (#23-36), one given and one taken each. Don't swap cards directly between two players. Keep both sides: public and private.
3. **PC build.** Normal 5e race, class, background and stats at level 5. Starting wealth and gear follow your edition's table for starting above 1st level. Because PCs are established, their desire, relationships and tags describe a history they already have.
4. **Starting reputation tags.** Each PC starts with 2 tags: one positive and one negative. Each tag names a skill and a scene (see section 5). The DM approves them.
5. **NPCs.** Each player builds 2 NPCs (see section 3).
6. **Introductions.** Each player introduces their PC. Authors introduce their NPCs. DM checks every PC has a desire, a relationship, two NPCs, two tags and at least one hook.

## 3. Player-Built NPCs (Connections)
- **Who:** each player builds 1 NPC for each of the other two PCs. Every PC ends up with 2 NPCs from 2 different authors.
- **Build:** draw cards from the Deck of Connections (#37-66), pick the a or b side, choose a relationship to the target PC, and fill in a sheet:
  - Name (the card gives only a title, such as "The Duelist"; you choose who they are)
  - Relationship to target PC
  - Opinion of target PC
  - Notes
  - **Want:** something the NPC needs
  - **Secret:** known only to author and DM
- **Control:** the author plays the NPC for the whole campaign.
- **Leverage:** each NPC has 2 Leverage tokens. The author spends them when the NPC does something story-significant (compels someone, reveals a secret, harms an interest). No automatic refresh. When someone compels or harms the NPC and the author agrees, the author gains a Leverage token. DM may top an NPC up to 1 at a milestone if it is empty.
- **Mid-campaign slot:** each player may create one more NPC later, for any PC.
- **Stats:** DM supplies a stat block when needed, scaled to the current tier of play.

### Guardrails
1. **Purpose:** an NPC's job is to complicate its target PC's life or be a tool in their plans.
2. **No self-dealing:** an NPC can't directly advance its author's own PC's desire without spending 1 Leverage.
3. **Consent for harm:** if an NPC does something harmful to its target PC and it matters to the story, the author spends Leverage and offers it to the target's player. The target can accept, accept with a condition ("yes, but only if..."), or refuse. If accepted, the target PC gains 1 Inspiration.
4. **Same scene:** if an author's PC and NPC are in the same scene, DM plays one of them. The author still decides how the NPC behaves.

## 4. Inspiration (replaces Resolve tokens)
- **Cap:** 3 per player.
- **Earn 1 when you:**
  - act on your desire in a way that complicates your life;
  - accept a harmful action from another player's NPC;
  - have a negative tag invoked against you, or volunteer to play into it (see section 5);
  - give a spent rumour a good scene.
- **Spend 1 to:**
  - gain advantage on a roll (standard 5e);
  - declare a story-significant detail (a carriage breaks down, a rival shows up at the ball). The DM has the final say, and if it harms another PC, use the consent rule in Guardrail 3;
  - compel a letter or an NPC action (see section 7).
- Minor details never cost Inspiration.

## 5. Reputation Tags
Tags are lasting traits. They describe how society sees a PC, and they amplify (or hamper) specific skills in specific scenes. They are not used up when they come into play.

### Anatomy of a tag
Every tag has four parts:
- **Word:** the label society uses ("Silver-tongued", "Hot-headed").
- **Skill:** one skill the tag affects (Persuasion, Insight, Intimidation, Arcana, Performance and so on).
- **Scene:** the kind of situation where it matters (at court, in the markets, among temple folk, on the battlefield, in the wilds, in the underworld).
- **Pips:** 1 to 3. Pips show how deeply the reputation is set. A new tag starts with 1.

*Examples*
- Positive: **Silver-tongued** (Persuasion, at court), 1 pip.
- Positive: **Battle-tested** (Intimidation, on the battlefield), 2 pips.
- Negative: **Hot-headed** (Deception, in negotiations), 1 pip.
- Negative: **Oathbreaker** (Persuasion, among temple folk), 2 pips.

The DM approves the Skill and Scene pairing so that tags stay specific.

### Positive tags: Amplify
- When the tag's scene is in play and you make a check with its skill, you may invoke the tag to gain **advantage** on that check.
- **Uses per session** equal the tag's pips (they refresh at the start of each session).
- **At 3 pips (Signature):** once per session, treat a d20 roll below 10 as a 10 on a check the tag covers.

### Negative tags: Weigh
- When the tag's scene is in play and the skill comes up, the DM or any other player may invoke the tag against you, giving you **disadvantage** on that check.
- It can be invoked against you up to its pips in times per session.
- **Each time it is invoked against you, you gain 1 Inspiration.** You can also volunteer to play into it for the same reward.
- You can't invoke your own negative tags to hurt yourself in order to farm Inspiration beyond that limit.

### Earning and deepening tags
- **When:** at each milestone checkpoint, each PC may earn **one** new tag or add **one** pip to an existing tag.
- **Criteria:**
  - **Positive** if you acted in service of duty or morality at a cost to your own desire, or kept to society's conventions despite real hardship.
  - **Negative** if you acted on your desire at the cost of duty or morality, or broke society's conventions.
- You can earn one of each in the same checkpoint.
- If you already have a tag that fits, deepen it instead of making a new one (max 3 pips).
- The group helps choose the Word, Skill and Scene, based on what actually happened.
- **Cap:** 3 positive and 3 negative tags at once. At the cap, you must deepen a tag or replace one.

### Shedding tags
- **Positive tags** can be lost if you act against them in a major way (the DM and player agree), or swapped at an Act Break.
- **Negative tags** are removed by in-fiction redemption: an atonement scene, a public apology, a quest or a favour. The DM and player agree when it is done. Removing a negative tag also reduces its pips, one at a time.
- Society remembers. The DM should reflect tags in how NPCs treat the PC.

## 6. Rumour Board
- **Purpose:** a shared list of gossip, written by the players as "society", not as their characters.
- **At the end of each session:** each player either creates a rumour (about a PC, NPC or the world) or spreads an existing one.
- **Spread rumours:** mark them with a Spark. Any player may spend a Spark once during a later session to make the rumour affect the story, with the same consent and DM rules as above. Then cross it off.
- **Fade:** an unspread rumour is marked as fading after one session and crossed off after the next.
- **Truth:** rumours don't have to be true, but they may become true.
- **Scale:** rumours grow with the campaign (see section 12). In Act I they are about Houses and neighbours. Later they can be about the Concord itself.

## 7. Letters Between Sessions
- Each player may write one in-character letter between sessions (to a PC, an NPC, or anyone).
- Letters can plant information, ask for a favour or confess something. Normal rules still apply to anything with consequences.
- The recipient's player replies at the start of the next session, or in chat.
- To compel a letter to be written to you, spend 1 Inspiration. The sender still writes the contents.

## 8. Monologue Token
- Each player gets one token per session.
- Spend it to ask another PC (or an NPC played by another player) a question: "What does she really think of Henry?"
- That player answers honestly, in character, aloud.
- An unspent token must be used at the end of the session.

## 9. Inner Conflict
- **Set:** at the end of session 1, each PC names an "X vs Y" tension (Love vs Wealth, Family vs Freedom).
- **Track:** at each milestone, mark a box for each significant choice in the last stretch of play that favoured a side.
- **Resolves** when one side has 3 marks, or there are 5 marks in total.
- **On resolution:**
  - Frame a short reflection scene.
  - Clear the conflict and create a new one (or reuse the old one if you remain torn).
  - Take one **Backstory action:** create a new NPC, change an NPC's fortune (reverse wealth or marital status, reveal a secret as a rumour, or give them a new desire), establish a new relationship, or replace your desire.

## 10. Milestone Checkpoint (replaces Upkeep)
Each time the party levels up (about 5-10 minutes):
1. Spend or lose your monologue token.
2. Earn or deepen one tag (section 5).
3. Update inner conflict marks.
4. Keep, replace or retire your desire.
5. Clean up the rumour board.
6. Top up empty NPC Leverage if you choose.

## 11. Concord Etiquette (social magic)
At level 5 and above, spells such as *zone of truth*, *detect thoughts*, *charm person*, *scrying* and *clairvoyance* can break intrigue. Setting rule:
- **Wards:** Houses protect their drawing rooms, ballrooms and council halls with truthstone wards. Inside a warded space, divination and enchantment on a guest fail, or alert the House.
- **Standing:** breaking or bypassing a ward is a serious breach of Standing. If it is discovered, the offender earns a negative tag at the next milestone (or deepens a matching one).
- **Exceptions:** a host may invite magic ("truth-binding" is a formal custom for oaths). A character with the right standing may be allowed to use a spell openly.
- **Outside the wards:** roads, inns, wilderness and dungeons follow normal spell rules. Social magic is something you do where society can't see you, with all the risk that implies.

## 12. Campaign Structure (levels 5-20)
Fifteen levels is long, so the campaign runs in four acts that follow 5e's tiers. Each act ends with an **Act Break**: a longer checkpoint that refreshes the Good Society layer before it goes stale.

| Act | Levels | Scale of play | Typical stakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 5-8 | Local: a few Houses, one region | Inheritance, marriages, feuds, the first threat |
| II | 9-12 | The capital and the great Houses of Corvane | Alliances, succession, treaties, a secret that threatens the realm |
| III | 13-16 | The realm and neighbouring powers | War, a crisis in the ward-network, rival realms |
| IV | 17-20 | The world and beyond | Planar threats, remaking the Concord, final reckonings |

**Pace:** with milestone levelling, plan for about 2-3 sessions per level, so roughly 30-45 sessions in total. Adjust to your group's schedule.

**Act Break (end of Acts I, II and III), about 20-30 minutes:**
1. Run a normal milestone checkpoint.
2. **Desires:** each player must either replace their desire or deepen it with a new twist. The old one is marked achieved, abandoned or failed.
3. **Reputation:** each player may rewrite or swap one tag (new Word, Skill or Scene), and may move one pip from one tag to another. Society's opinion shifts as the scale of play grows.
4. **Rumour board:** clear it and start fresh.
5. **NPCs:** each player may change one NPC's fortune, retire one NPC and create a replacement, or reset its Leverage to 2.
6. **Inner conflict:** resolve it if it is mostly full, or reset it.
7. **PC succession (optional):** a player may retire their PC and bring in a new one at the same level. The old PC becomes an NPC they still control, as in the base game.
8. **Investment check:** ask everyone if they still want to continue, as Good Society recommends. Reaching an Act Break is a good point to finish if the group is tired.

**Final session (level 20):** skip the rumour board. Each player closes with an **epilogue letter** written by their PC, set years after the last scene, describing what became of them and their NPCs. Then title the story together.

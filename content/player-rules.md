# Player Cheat Sheet

**The short version:** we play D&D 5e as normal. On top of it, the Concord layer tracks who you are, who you're tied to and what society says about you. Everything here is a quick reference; ask the DM for the full detail.

## 1. Your Turn at the Table
- **Fights, checks, spells, exploring:** normal 5e.
- **Your desire:** a secret card you must act on. Only the DM knows it unless you choose to share.
- **Your relationships:** the ties you gave and took in the ring. You choose which are public and which private on your "My cards" page.

## 2. Inspiration
You can hold **up to 3**.

| Earn 1 when you | Spend 1 to |
|---|---|
| Act on your desire in a way that complicates your life | Gain advantage on a roll |
| Accept a harmful action from another player's NPC | Declare a story-significant detail (DM has the final say) |
| Have a negative tag invoked against you (or play into it) | Compel a letter or an NPC action |
| Give a spent rumour a good scene | |

Small details never cost Inspiration.

## 3. Reputation Tags
A tag is how society sees you: **Word** (the label), **Skill**, **Scene** (where it matters) and **Pips** (1-3, how deep it runs).

| | Positive tag | Negative tag |
|---|---|---|
| In its scene, on its skill | You may invoke it for **advantage** | DM or another player may invoke it for **disadvantage** against you |
| Uses per session | Equal to pips | Equal to pips |
| Bonus | At 3 pips: once a session, treat a d20 under 10 as a 10 | You gain **1 Inspiration** each time it's invoked |

- You start with 1 positive and 1 negative tag (DM approves them).
- At each **milestone** you may earn one new tag or add one pip. Positive for duty over desire; negative for desire over duty. Deepen a fitting tag instead of making a duplicate.
- Maximum 3 positive and 3 negative tags.
- Lose a negative tag through in-story redemption. Lose a positive one by acting against it in a big way.

## 4. Your NPCs (Connections)
- You build **2 NPCs**, one for each other PC, and play them for the whole campaign.
- Each has **2 Leverage tokens**. Spend one when the NPC does something story-significant (compel, reveal a secret, harm an interest). No automatic refresh.
- An NPC exists to complicate its target's life or be a tool in their plans. It can't directly help your own PC's desire without spending Leverage.
- If an NPC harms its target in a way that matters, the target's player can say yes, yes-but, or no. If yes, they gain 1 Inspiration.
- You get **one extra NPC** to create later in the campaign.

## 5. Rumours
- At the end of each session, **write a new rumour or spread an existing one** (you write as "society", not your character).
- A spread rumour gets a **Spark**. Spend a Spark once in a later session to make it affect the story, then cross it off.
- Unspread rumours fade after one session and are removed after the next. Rumours need not be true.

## 6. Letters and Monologues
- **Letters:** one in-character letter between sessions, to anyone. The recipient replies next session.
- **Monologue token:** one per session. Ask another PC (or a player-run NPC) a question; they must answer honestly, in character. Use it or lose it at session's end.

## 7. Inner Conflict
- After session 1, name an "X vs Y" tension for your PC (Love vs Wealth, Family vs Freedom).
- At each milestone, mark a box for significant choices that favoured a side.
- It resolves at 3 marks on one side or 5 in total. Then play a short reflection scene, take a new conflict and one **Backstory action** (new NPC, change an NPC's fortune, new relationship, or replace your desire).

## 8. Concord Etiquette
- Great Houses protect their halls with **truthstone wards**. Divination and enchantment on guests fails or alerts the House.
- Bypassing a ward is a breach of **Standing**. If discovered, expect a negative tag at the next milestone.
- Outside the wards (roads, inns, wilds, dungeons), spells work normally.

## 9. Milestone Checklist
Each time we level up:
1. Spend or lose your monologue token.
2. Earn or deepen one tag.
3. Update inner conflict marks.
4. Keep, replace or retire your desire.
5. Quick tone check-in.
6. Tidy the rumour board.

**Act Breaks** (levels 8, 12, 16) are longer: you also replace or deepen your desire, may rewrite one tag, and may change an NPC. At level 20, you write an epilogue letter for your PC.
